**Reviewed and reconciled with current code:** 2026-10-18
**Scope:** the current repository's study UI, guide reader and generator, quizzes and practice tests, flashcards, tutor, and progress views. This is a code and design review, not a usability study, accessibility conformance audit, or proof that generated material is factually correct. ## AI study performance score — implemented with limits

After a released quiz or exam result, or after a learner rates flashcards, the browser requests a private study score from `POST /api/study-materials/{material_id}/performance-score`. The backend builds a class-level summary from at most the 20 most recent released runs in each assessment category and the latest saved ratings from at most 30 readable decks. It sends counts and numeric scores/ratings to the configured provider, not question text, answer choices, written responses, chat, flashcard reflections, or other learners' activity. A model response must include the score components supported by evidence; deterministic validation rejects scores that diverge substantially from the observed results. The backend, rather than the model, calculates the composite.

Quizzes and practice exams are the primary performance evidence. When both are available they each contribute 45%; flashcard practice contributes 10%. If only one assessment category is available with flashcards, that assessment contributes 90% and flashcards 10%. Without flashcards, available assessment categories share the full composite. Flashcard-only activity is shown as a practice signal, with no composite. This fixed lower weight reflects that card ratings are self-reported rehearsal, not assessment results. The number of attempts is shown as an **evidence level**, not statistically calibrated confidence.

The score is advisory, not a course grade, mastery estimate, or clinical-readiness claim. It is private to the learner, is excluded from the mastery estimator and instructor views, is cached for identical evidence, and can be cleared by class. The saved record contains only the scores, short explanation, aggregate counts, and model/prompt provenance. Provider errors leave quiz, exam, and flashcard records unchanged and can be retried. Focused tests use a fake provider; no paid provider generation or learner study has validated score usefulness yet. Before using these scores for consequential decisions, evaluate them against held-out cases and human-reviewed expectations; do not treat the model score as more authoritative than the underlying quiz results.

## What “best practice” means here

No single study interface is best for every learner or subject. The strongest general evidence supports **retrieving information from memory, revisiting it over time, and receiving useful feedback**. Worked examples, mixed practice, and self-explanation help when matched to the learner and task. Interface quality also requires accessible controls and clear information architecture. These are design hypotheses for Jot until delayed learning outcomes and usability are measured in this product. [Dunlosky et al., 2013](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/pspi/1529100612453266/); [IES study practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1); [IES postsecondary technology guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/25).

The product already has important pieces: source-linked generation and warnings, section-level guide checks for newly generated guides, short-answer questions, quiz explanations, optional reasoning, private concept estimates, scheduled short reviews, and private flashcard ratings and due dates. See [product design](design.md), [study-guide quality principles](study-guide-quality-principles.md), [`material-viewer.js`](../frontend/src/material-viewer.js), [`quiz-player.js`](../frontend/src/quiz-player.js), and [`progress.js`](../frontend/src/progress.js). The proposals below extend these existing paths. Recheck them before implementation because an audit finding can become stale as the code changes.

## Recommended experience by surface

### Study home and generation

1. Keep **Continue**, **Review due**, and **Create practice** together near the top. Continue must identify the student's last opened, unfinished material; otherwise label it **Latest material**. Show the selected class, source scope, and why each suggestion appears. Keep private progress estimates labeled as estimates. Retain advanced generation controls and source preview under a clear Customize action. This hierarchy is a product hypothesis to validate with task tests. [IES postsecondary guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/25).
2. Let a learner choose a concrete goal such as “understand a new topic,” “remember for next week,” or “simulate an exam.” Use that choice to recommend a guide plus a later check, a short review, or a timed test. State what each mode does. Avoid presenting one content format as the universal best choice. [EEF metacognition guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/metacognition).
3. Explain source coverage before generation: selected uploads, unavailable/failed sources, and whether excerpts were partial. Keep existing revision and missing-source warnings visible at the point of study. Generated content should distinguish course-source claims from outside explanation and uncertainty. [Existing product contract](design.md#notes-and-study-materials).

### Cross-cutting UI behavior

1. Give each screen one clear next action and keep labels consistent: **Study guide**, **Practice quiz**, **Exam simulation**, and **Flashcards** should mean the same thing in generation, saved materials, results, and recommendations. Put the primary action and status in the first viewport. For long pages, add a compact table of contents, section jump, search, or local action bar as appropriate; the student should not need to traverse a long feed to resume, inspect a cited source, or ask for help. Preserve keyboard focus and reading position across these jumps. This is an interface proposal to validate with tasks, not a measured Jot effect. [WCAG Consistent Navigation and Identification](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).
2. In generation, upload processing, grading, and guide preview, show what is happening, whether the student can leave, and the next recovery action on failure. Keep draft answers and already completed work through recoverable errors. Confirm destructive deletion and provide an undo path where feasible. Check status messages with a screen reader rather than relying on color or animation. [WCAG 2.2 status messages, error identification, and error prevention](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).
3. Make the main learning action usable at phone width and at large text sizes without horizontal scrolling. Keep reading lines comfortable, controls large enough to tap, and the cited source close to the claim or feedback it supports. An alternate text view should carry the same teaching meaning as a diagram. [WCAG Reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html); [WCAG Target Size](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); [CAST UDL Guidelines](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/).

### Study guides, diagrams, and activities

1. Start each section with a learner-facing question or outcome, a short explanation, then a worked example where the task is procedural. Prompt a prediction or a “why does this step follow?” response before showing the next step; fade scaffolding as competence is demonstrated. Pair worked examples with a similar practice problem rather than making the guide only a reading experience. [IES recommendations 2–4](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/20072004.pdf); [worked-example sequencing study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31465546/).
2. Preserve the current mini-quiz requirement and finalization repair, but add **varied** recall, application, and diagnosis prompts across sections. Give an explanation after an attempt. A retry of the identical item is useful for correction but is not independent evidence of learning. Link the section to a later, different question family. [Butler, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019902); [existing guide principles](study-guide-quality-principles.md).
3. Require every figure or activity to answer a named teaching question. Keep explanatory text beside the relevant visual elements, with a concise text equivalent. Test actual browser and PDF renders at phone width and enlarged text; the [BIO170 audit](study-guide-quality-principles.md#phone-scale-svg-text-check-2026-10-17) found visible labels under 8 CSS pixels in many stored SVGs. The current text-size audit is valuable but cannot detect wrong arrows, clipping, or factual errors. [CAST UDL Guidelines](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/); [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).
4. Keep animations paused initially and provide play, pause, and restart as the current design specifies. For a changing process, let learners inspect a meaningful still frame and read a static explanation. Check reduced motion in the browser and exported documents. [WCAG Pause, Stop, Hide](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide); [guide principles](study-guide-quality-principles.md).

### Quizzes and practice exams

1. Keep the current source-linked mix of multiple-choice, true/false, and short-answer items, but map each item to one learning objective and test a range of recall, interpretation, and application that the selected sources support. Prefer plausible misconception distractors. For repeated practice, use a different item family to check the same objective. [IES study practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/20072004.pdf); [transfer review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29733621/).
2. Preserve the existing **Practice** and **Exam simulation** distinction in the start screen. Practice should be low stakes and untimed; simulation can use its adjustable timer and delayed answer release. Check the one-minute warning and auto-submit with keyboard and assistive technology, and keep an untimed path. Never reveal an answer key early when class policy withholds it. [WCAG Timing Adjustable](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/timing-adjustable.html); [IES feedback recommendation](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/25).
3. In results, show **what was right or wrong, why, the cited source, and one next action**. Current released results already show much of this; make the next action explicit and use a new check after review. Keep the existing distinction between reasoning sufficiency and correctness. Do not force long written reasoning for routine recall; Reasoning Mode is optional and should stay so. [IES study practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/20072004.pdf); [reasoning-mode contract](reasoning-first-quiz-implementation.md).
4. Audit deterministic short-answer matching before using results as learning evidence: [`quiz_attempts.py`](../backend/app/api/quiz_attempts.py) compares normalized strings, while [`study_prompts.py`](../backend/app/services/study_prompts.py) asks for canonical and accepted answers. Add representative paraphrases, notation, units, and benign typo cases to item review; route ambiguous or subjective items away from automatic high-confidence grading. A correct idea marked wrong can misdirect practice.

### Flashcards and return visits

1. On the front, ask one answerable question; prompt the learner to **try to recall before revealing**. Keep the current keyboard shortcuts and source links. The existing authoring prompt already asks for atomic, varied cards; test generated decks for overloaded prompts and duplicate objectives. [Flashcard authoring prompt](../backend/app/services/study_prompts.py); [Dunlosky et al., 2013](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/pspi/1529100612453266/).
2. Preserve the existing **Recalled**, **Unsure**, and **Missed** labels and private scheduling. Test whether learners understand that these are self-reports; retain the separation from graded concept mastery. [Current flashcard player](../frontend/src/flashcards.js#L405); [current mastery model](design.md#source-grounded-learning-and-private-mastery).
3. Surface due cards in the Progress review queue and study entry flow, and make snooze/reset/clear easy to find. Progress now provides the due-deck queue and a due-only launch path; the per-card snooze/reset/clear controls remain in the deck. The current schedule is a planning aid; evaluate it with delayed recall on fresh questions, not only daily engagement. [Cepeda et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/).

### Tutor and progress

1. Preserve the current adaptive hint policy: ask for the student's current step when useful, give a focused hint, explain a missing prerequisite, then use a small analogous worked example after unsuccessful hints. The tutoring prompt already encodes this; evaluate it with real novice and advanced tasks before making it more restrictive. Avoid answers that solve the student's target step when a hint suffices. [Product tutor policy](design.md#tutor-style); [IES worked-example guidance](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/20072004.pdf).
2. Keep XP, recent accuracy, evidence sufficiency, and proficiency estimates distinct, as [`progress.js`](../frontend/src/progress.js) and the [product design](design.md#source-grounded-learning-and-private-mastery) intend. Put **what to do next** ahead of aggregate XP on the study entry path; retain uncertainty and source rationale. A 90-day age flag should read as a reminder, not as proof of forgetting. [EEF metacognition guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/metacognition).
3. Treat the concept graph as a navigation and diagnostic aid. Recommend prerequisites only from validated edges, and phrase downstream mistakes as a reason to check a foundation, not proof that the prerequisite is missing. This matches the current [learning recommendation logic](../backend/app/api/learning.py).

## Generated-content presentation contract

Apply the same quality bar to live tutor replies, guide sections, mini-quizzes, quiz feedback, flashcards, and exported documents. The backend chooses the allowed output structure and validates model responses; the browser renders only the supported subset. Reuse the existing math, guide-text, and SVG sanitizers where they fit. A reply that fails rich rendering should show readable escaped text and a clear error state, never executable HTML or a blank answer.

| Content | Required behavior | Verification fixture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prose, lists, and tables | Preserve paragraphs, hierarchy, list order, and table headers. Avoid raw Markdown delimiters or pipe tables in a surface that cannot render them; use a labeled-list fallback. | A multi-step explanation with nested list, comparison, long words, and screen-reader reading order. |
| Mathematics and chemistry | Typeset supported notation with accessible text/MathML, keep units and assumptions beside equations, and leave malformed expressions legible. Treat ordinary hyphenated words and currency as prose. | Fractions, subscripts, reaction arrows, matrices, units, Unicode symbols, malformed TeX, and a chemical structure with a text equivalent. |
| Code and traces | Escape code, preserve whitespace, identify language, and keep output separate from source. Use syntax highlighting or visualization only where it explains the concept; generated code is never executed as UI markup. | Long line wrapping, copy/select, an error trace, and a step-by-step algorithm at phone width. |
| Figures, SVG, and animation | Require a teaching purpose, source-supported labels and arrows, bounded viewBox, visible caption, and equivalent text. Sanitize SVG on server and browser. Review start, middle, and end states; provide play/pause/restart and reduced-motion fallback. | Rendered desktop, 360-pixel card, 320-CSS-pixel reflow, zoom, and PDF snapshots; inspect clipping, overlap, label size, arrow endpoints, and scientific meaning. |

Existing [`audit_svg_phone_text`](../backend/app/services/study_guide_visual_preview.py#L61) and author self-preview are useful checks, but a model checking its own diagram cannot establish correctness. Add an independent rendered-artifact review for high-risk diagrams and inspect saved guides that predate the new audit. Compare browser and PDF output for the same fixture. Subject libraries such as the existing chemistry, charting, graph, and code packages may be extended when they improve the explanation; load heavy modules on demand and keep a readable fallback.

## Website design and discoverability gate

Use a small shared design system for study surfaces: consistent type scale, spacing, colors, button roles, focus states, status messages, cards, and empty/error states. Keep the main action visible without deep scrolling on a typical laptop and phone viewport. A long guide needs persistent progress and a section jump; a quiz result needs a direct route to explanation, source, and next practice; a due review needs an entry point from Study as well as Progress. The floating guide chat should sit near the current passage, avoid covering it, and move to a usable sheet on narrow screens.

Review representative pages with real-length titles, dense content, loading and failure states, light/dark themes, 200%/400% zoom, reduced motion, keyboard navigation, and a screen reader. Check [WCAG 2.2 reflow, focus visibility, and target size](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) as measurable constraints. Record task success for finding Continue, Review due, Ask about this section, source evidence, and the next action after a missed question. Visual coherence and reachability are release criteria.

## Accessibility and content quality gate

Target **WCAG 2.2 AA** for the browser study flows, including the guide, quiz, flashcards, generation form, source viewer, progress, and live tutor. Check keyboard order, visible focus, labels and status announcements, 200%/400% zoom and reflow, text and control contrast, pointer target size, text alternatives for diagrams, and reduced motion. Use native controls where possible. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); [CAST UDL Guidelines](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/). The CSS contains focus and reduced-motion rules, and the flashcard flip surface now uses a native [`button`](../frontend/src/flashcards.js#L430); check their behavior in actual browsers before claiming conformance.

For AI-authored material, add a review protocol with two independent questions: **Does the cited course source support the claim?** and **Is the claim/answer scientifically or mathematically sound?** Check generated answer keys, accepted answers, calculations, worked steps, diagram labels/arrows, accessible alternatives, and PDF parity. Record conflicts with course materials and provide a route for instructor or student correction. Schema validity, a citation, and a model's self-review are insufficient on their own; this is demonstrated in the [BIO170 audit](study-guide-quality-principles.md). Treat source files and live media according to the existing [privacy boundaries](design.md#privacy-and-data-handling). Minimal private review metadata may be stored for scheduling and resuming with a clear/reset path; chat text, free-text answers, screen media, and audio remain unsaved.

## How to validate the changes

1. **Learning outcomes:** compare delayed, source-aligned checks after 1–4 weeks for students who use a new review path versus the current path. Include fresh items and measure completion, correctness, and transfer separately. Same-session score and time spent are secondary. Avoid interpreting a higher click rate or more XP as learning. [Dunlosky et al., 2013](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/pspi/1529100612453266/); [quizzing meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33683913/).
2. **Content quality:** sample by subject and format; report unsupported claims, wrong keys, ambiguous questions, citation mismatches, source conflicts, unreadable diagrams, and incorrect feedback per 100 items/sections. Give reviewers the exact source revision and rendered browser/PDF artifact. Recheck after source edits. [Existing source and quality contract](design.md#source-grounded-learning-and-private-mastery).
3. **Usability and access:** run task-based sessions at desktop and phone widths with keyboard and screen-reader users. Tasks: begin a due review, finish a guide check, understand a wrong answer, resume a quiz, adjust a timer, inspect a source, and recover from a failed generation. Record task success and observed barriers; test both light/dark and reduced-motion settings. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).
4. **Privacy:** use aggregate outcome counts where possible. Do not collect raw Live screen frames, audio, or conversation text to evaluate study improvements; existing product rules forbid that storage. [Product privacy contract](design.md#privacy-and-data-handling).

The first code slice now joins a guide section to clarification, a submitted mini-quiz, corrective feedback, and a private due date while preserving answer-key withholding. The later chat-generated practice is intentionally unscored until a validated question-family mapping is available. Next work is the authorized fresh-question bridge, subject-matter review of generated content, and learner evaluation—not a claim that the learning loop has been proven.
