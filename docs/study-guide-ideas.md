# Study guide ideas

**Drafted and expanded:** 2026-10-18. These are product proposals to test, not measured improvements in Jot. They build on the current [guide reader](../frontend/src/material-viewer.js), [style picker](../frontend/src/study.js), and [study-guide quality principles](study-guide-quality-principles.md).

## Current ways guides can hurt learning

1. **Students may learn incorrect facts or relationships.** A guide can be marked ready after [structure and export checks](../backend/app/api/class_library.py#L649) without checking whether its claims, calculations, or diagrams agree with the sources. The [BIO170 review](study-guide-quality-principles.md#findings-from-the-2026-10-17-bio170-review) found examples of source conflicts and misleading figures.
2. **Reading can replace retrieval practice.** A read-only audit of the local database on 2026-10-18 found that all four ready guides have zero section `mini_quiz` entries. [Legacy revalidation](../backend/app/api/class_library.py#L784) leaves those checks absent, so these guides offer no built-in way to test recall after a section.
3. **The retry can create false confidence.** After a wrong answer, the [reader](../frontend/src/material-viewer.js#L203) reveals the correct choice and then lets the student retry the *same* question. Getting it right after seeing the answer says little about whether they can recall or apply it later.
4. **A citation may no longer verify the claim.** The [citation link](../frontend/src/source-citations.js#L53) opens the current upload, even when the source changed after the guide was generated. That can prevent students from resolving a discrepancy and leave an outdated claim unchallenged.
5. **Figures may be hard to interpret on phones.** The [saved BIO170 audit](study-guide-quality-principles.md#phone-scale-svg-text-check-2026-10-17) found text below 8 CSS pixels in 59 of 69 SVGs. Tiny labels and unclear connectors can make a correct explanation difficult to learn or suggest the wrong relationship.

## Five broader opportunities

1. **A clear study path:** put due review, a challenging concept, and the actual last activity on the study home screen. The current “Continue studying” action selects the newest ready material ([`study.js`](../frontend/src/study.js#L856)).
2. **A complete feedback loop:** connect a guide check or missed quiz item to its source, a useful explanation, and a different question later.
3. **Flexible explanations:** offer concise text, detailed reasoning, diagrams with text alternatives, and worked examples according to the topic and learner choice. Avoid assigning fixed “learning style” labels; evidence does not support matching instruction to such labels ([Pashler et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x)).
4. **Mixed practice:** after initial teaching, mix related problem types so learners choose a method rather than repeat one pattern.
5. **Trustworthy material:** check generated answer keys, calculations, claims, and diagrams against their cited sources, and make error reporting easy.

The [IES study practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) supports spaced review, quizzing, worked examples, and combining graphics with explanation. Its [postsecondary technology guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/25) supports varied resources, self-regulated study, and timely targeted feedback. These sources support the learning mechanisms; they do not establish that a particular Jot UI will improve outcomes.

## Guide-first feature candidates

| Priority | Feature | Concrete experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Teach → recall → explain → retry** | Each section starts with one learning objective. After the explanation, the learner attempts a brief free-response or decision question before seeing feedback and the source. A later check uses a different question family. The current check is one multiple-choice item with in-memory state ([`material-viewer.js`](../frontend/src/material-viewer.js#L203)). |
| 2 | **Worked-example ladder** | For procedural topics, show one fully worked example, then a similar example with a missing step, then an independent problem. Make the learner explain *why* a step applies. Keep this optional for topics that are better taught another way. |
| 3 | **Section-level format controls** | Let readers switch between “quick overview,” “walk me through it,” and “show an example” within a guide, with a diagram beside the relevant words and a usable text equivalent. | 4 | **Guide-to-review bridge** | Save which sections were studied and which checks were missed; offer fresh source-linked retrieval after a delay. The reader already saves a section position locally ([`material-viewer.js`](../frontend/src/material-viewer.js#L588)), but its check state lasts only for the open viewer. Keep self-ratings separate from graded mastery evidence. |
| 5 | **Evidence and correction panel** | Put the exact cited page, slide, or timestamp beside a claim; disclose partial source coverage; allow “this seems wrong” on a paragraph, answer, or figure. Before release, sample high-risk facts, keys, calculations, and visuals for source agreement. The finalization path currently runs deterministic structure/export checks ([`class_library.py`](../backend/app/api/class_library.py#L649)); an independent review method exists in the service but is not called there ([`gemini_content.py`](../backend/app/services/gemini_content.py#L2822)). A model review still needs human spot checks. |
| 6 | **Better return experience** | On reopening, show the last section, a short recap, and a choice to continue or try a fresh recall check. Make guide completion reflect attempted learning actions rather than pages viewed. |

## Guide learning flow to build

1. **Orient:** At the top of each section, show one question the learner should be able to answer. Keep section progress and a jump menu visible; offer a direct route back to the last guide and section from Study. Keep the guide's original source scope visible.
2. **Teach:** Put a short explanation beside the relevant diagram or formula. For a procedure, show a worked example, then a partially worked step, then a new problem. Section-level controls can request a concise recap, a slower explanation, or another example without regenerating the whole guide. These are learner choices, not fixed “learning styles.”
3. **Retrieve:** Require an attempt before revealing feedback. An identical retry after the answer is shown is a correction exercise; later independent evidence must use a new prompt or question family. Older saved guides with no mini-quizzes need an honest “No section check yet” state and a path to a fresh check, or a reviewed backfill. The local audit found four ready guides with no section checks.
4. **Return:** Offer a source-linked review after a delay through the existing private [short-review path](../backend/app/api/learning.py#L379) where the guide section can be mapped to a validated concept/question family. If that mapping is unavailable, show a practice suggestion without inventing a mastery score. Save only the minimum private progress metadata needed for resuming and scheduling; keep free-text attempts and chat out of persistent history by default.
5. **Correct:** A student can flag a specific paragraph, answer, or figure. Show what claim is in question, its original cited locator, the source-change state, and whether a correction is pending. Do not silently rewrite a guide that other students may already be using; release a corrected revision and keep its PDF synchronized.

## Floating chat for a study guide

**Purpose:** Let a student resolve confusion without leaving the passage they are studying, then check whether they can use the idea themselves.

- **Reader experience:** A small **Ask about this section** button stays inside the guide viewer. It opens a side panel on desktop and a bottom sheet on phones, leaving the section readable. A selection or block-level **Explain this** action can point the chat at a particular sentence, formula, or figure. The panel shows the active section and offers **Explain more simply**, **Show an example**, **How does this connect?**, and **Quiz me** starters. It remains available while navigating, but labels previous messages with their section so context changes are clear. Closing it returns keyboard focus to the trigger.
- **Teaching behavior:** Answer a clarification directly and briefly, then offer one concrete example or a check for understanding. For a practice request, ask a *new* question, wait for the student's attempt, and give specific feedback; do not count a repeated, answer-revealed item as evidence of learning. If the student asks about a guide mini-quiz before attempting it, give a hint without exposing its key. The chat should help a stuck student without turning every question into a long Socratic exchange.
- **Grounding and trust:** The backend loads the saved guide section and a small set of relevant, currently authorized course-source excerpts. Responses link to the guide passage and available source page, slide, or timestamp. If evidence is missing, truncated, or changed since generation, say so and avoid pretending the cited source confirms the claim. Let the student flag a suspect answer or diagram. Treat source and guide text as reference data, never as instructions to the tutor.
- **Scope and privacy:** Start with text chat scoped to the current section; let the student ask for a connection to another section or the whole guide. Keep chat history in memory for the open viewer and clear it when the viewer closes. Do not save messages or use them as mastery evidence by default. Keep provider selection and prompts on the backend; every request rechecks account and guide access.
- **Implementation slice:** Add a guide-chat endpoint taking the material ID, section index, optional block index, and bounded message history. The server resolves the actual guide and sources; the browser sends no answer keys or source text. Reuse the existing [quiz-review tutor panel](../frontend/src/study-tutor.js) patterns for focus, cancellation, retry, and limits. Its [current endpoint](../backend/app/api/study_tutor.py#L242) requires a quiz attempt, so the guide needs a separate request contract. Test whether students can answer a *new* question after a clarification, rather than measuring chat volume alone.

### Concrete interaction

The student opens a section, selects an unfamiliar formula, and chooses **Explain this**. The panel names the formula and section. Jot gives a two-to-five-sentence explanation, defines symbols and units, and links the available course page. **Show an example** uses a different set of values and leaves the student's next step to try. **Quiz me** asks a new question, waits for an answer, then explains the result and offers a later fresh check. If the source conflicts with the guide or has changed, Jot identifies the uncertainty and offers **Flag this passage**.

### Proposed request and response contract

Use a same-origin endpoint such as `POST /api/study-materials/{material_id}/guide-chat`. A request contains `section_index`, optional `block_index` and selection offsets, `scope` (`section` or `guide`), `intent` (`explain`, `example`, `connect`, or `practice`), and bounded alternating messages. The browser supplies identifiers and the student's words; the backend extracts selected text from the saved guide and rejects out-of-range selections. Every turn checks the user's material access, resolves the current guide version, and retrieves only relevant authorized source excerpts. A guide-wide question retrieves a bounded set of sections instead of stuffing a 100-section guide into the model context.

Return validated display blocks plus references and source-status warnings. Supported blocks should cover paragraph, ordered/unordered list, math, code, and a figure or existing-guide-block reference. A generated SVG needs a title, visible caption, text alternative, source reference, compact viewBox, and a plain-text fallback. The server sanitizes and bounds it; the browser sanitizes again before insertion. Model-authored scripts, HTML, links to arbitrary resources, and application-control actions are excluded. Keep code as displayed text unless a separately reviewed activity explicitly runs it. When a reply cannot be rendered safely, show escaped text and an actionable retry rather than an empty panel. Existing math and SVG renderers are a starting point; chemistry, code, chart, or graph libraries can be added for a specific teaching need and loaded on demand.

Before a learner submits a guide mini-quiz answer, omit its key and explanation from chat context and give hints for that item. For **Quiz me**, keep the generated prompt, the student's reply, and feedback as a transient chat exercise; it is not validated question-bank evidence or a mastery update. The backend should distinguish “the guide says,” “the cited source says,” and an extra explanatory inference. Current guide citations are not pinned to source revisions, so a changed-source warning must travel with the chat response until revision-accurate citations are implemented.

### Interaction and release checks

- Desktop panel and phone sheet preserve the current reading position; opening, closing, section changes, retries, and errors do not obscure the source passage or lose keyboard focus. Escape closes the panel; labels, status announcements, and a bounded conversation log work with a screen reader. The launch control remains near the active section, without requiring a deep scroll.
- Check long equations, chemical notation, code blocks, lists, tables or labeled-list fallbacks, and SVGs at desktop and 320 CSS pixels / 400% zoom. Inspect PDF/browser parity for any diagram shared with guide export. Review animated start, middle, and end states plus reduced motion. Sanitization and a minimum text-size diagnostic are necessary but do not prove a figure's scientific meaning.
- Exercise a source edit, deleted upload, revoked class membership, inaccessible assessment answer key, 100-section guide, malformed provider reply, provider timeout, and rate limit. The chat should preserve privacy, show a recoverable state, and never fabricate a citation or a successful save.
- Measure whether the learner can answer a different question later and whether they can resolve a source discrepancy. Conversation count and panel opens are usage measures, not learning outcomes.

**First chat prototype:** one ready guide section with an accessible floating text panel, a source-linked clarification, and a fresh retrieval question after the explanation. Compare accuracy on a different question later with the current guide flow; track incorrect-content reports and phone-width usability. Minimal private review metadata for scheduling and resuming is in scope; broader analytics or saved conversations need a separate privacy decision under the [product design](design.md#privacy-and-data-handling).
